About Museum Globe

동시대 유물 지구본은 한국과 중국에서 같은 시기에 만들어진 유물을 지구본 위에 나란히 놓고 비교하는 웹 지도입니다. 국립중앙박물관, 베이징 고궁박물원, 뉴욕 메트로폴리탄 미술관(The Met), 런던 빅토리아 앤드 앨버트 박물관(V&A)의 소장품 1,228점을 담고 있습니다.

무엇을 볼 수 있나요

  • 12세기: 고려청자와 송나라의 회화·도자 1,125점
  • 14~19세기: 조선 백자·청화백자와 명·청 백자·청화 자기 103점. 같은 종류의 자기를 한국·중국의 박물관과 영국 V&A, 미국 The Met 소장품으로 함께 볼 수 있습니다.

보는 방법

  • 아래 세기 막대로 시기를 고릅니다. 막대 아래 점은 유물이 있는 세기이고, ‹ › 버튼은 유물이 있는 세기로 바로 이동합니다.
  • 지구본의 점이나 오른쪽 목록을 누르면 사진, 연대, 소장처와 박물관 원문 링크가 나옵니다.
  • 검색과 한국·중국 버튼, 분류, 도자 종류(청자·백자·청화백자)로 유물을 거를 수 있습니다.
  • 빨간 점은 한국 문화권, 청록 점은 중국 문화권 유물입니다. 지구본은 끌어서 돌리고 휠로 확대합니다.

읽을 때 알아 둘 점

  • 상세 정보의 연대 근거는 연대가 박물관 기록에서 왔는지, 왕조 범위로 추정했는지, 비교를 위해 배치했는지를 알려 줍니다. 예를 들어 12세기 고려청자 중 1,045점은 세기 표기가 없어 비교용으로 12세기에 둔 것입니다.
  • 지도 위치는 대부분 시대·문화권별 추정 위치입니다. 박물관 기록에 가마가 적힌 38점만 그 가마에 놓았습니다. 같은 자리의 점을 흩어 놓은 모양에는 지리적 의미가 없습니다.
  • 모든 기록은 각 박물관의 공식 소장품 페이지로 연결됩니다. 데이터 출처와 한계는 About(영문)에 자세히 적었습니다.

Museum Globe is a prototype web map that places Korean and Chinese objects of the same period side by side. It holds 1,228 records from four museums: 1,088 from the National Museum of Korea, 77 from the Palace Museum, Beijing, 37 from The Metropolitan Museum of Art, New York, and 26 from the Victoria and Albert Museum, London. The first 1,125 records form a 12th-century comparison of Goryeo celadon and Song works. A sample of 103 white and blue-and-white porcelains from the Joseon, Ming and Qing periods (14th–19th centuries) was added in October 2026.

Dates

Not every record carries an individual 12th-century date. 23 Korean records are dated to the 12th century on the museum's page; the other 1,045 are Goryeo celadons whose pages give no century, placed in the 12th century for comparison. The 57 Chinese records are dated by dynasty (Northern Song, Southern Song or Song) and trimmed to the 12th century, so some may fall outside it.

In the porcelain sample, 79 records carry a date from the museum's own record: a reign period such as Yongle (1403–1424) or a year range. The other 24, including all 20 from the National Museum of Korea, are dated only to the Joseon, Ming or Qing dynasty and appear across that whole span.

Sources and images

All records were collected from the official online collection pages and APIs of the four museums, and each record links back to its source page. Images are loaded directly from the museums' websites. Images from the National Museum of Korea are recorded as KOGL Type 1. Images from the Palace Museum are linked from its official website; this dataset records no licence for them. Images from the V&A are credited to the museum and shown under its terms for non-commercial use. Images from The Met are in the public domain (CC0) under its Open Access policy. Licences have not yet been checked image by image.

Records

Each object has structured fields for its name and its name in Chinese characters, date, what the date is based on, dynasty, material, category, ware (celadon, white or blue-and-white), holding museum, image licence and source. The records are a selected sample gathered by a script, not a complete count of any collection. The 103 porcelains were chosen after review from 253 candidates, about ten per museum and ware, leaving out duplicates, fragments and pieces with other decoration. Only three white pieces at the V&A from each country met the criteria.

Map positions

Places on the map are estimated locations assigned by period and culture. All Korean records are placed at the Gangjin kiln area, a major centre of Goryeo celadon production. Chinese records are placed at the Song capitals, Kaifeng (Northern Song) and Hangzhou (Southern Song), or at a general point in eastern China. Porcelain is placed at the Gwangju royal kilns (Joseon) or at Jingdezhen (Ming and Qing) as an estimate, except 38 pieces whose museum record names the kiln: 29 at Jingdezhen and 9 white wares at Dehua. Points that share a location are spread out so each can be selected; the spread has no geographic meaning. Apart from those 38, positions are not yet object-by-object findspots or kiln sites, and refining them is the next step.

Status

Built in June 2026 by Nayun Kim as a self-directed exercise in digital archiving, using AI-assisted coding. The Joseon, Ming and Qing porcelain sample, drawn from four museums, was added in October 2026. Next are Korean metalwork and painting.
